⬜ St Peter’s Square

The colonnade, the obelisk, a security line that is the other monument. Morning. The square is free. The queue is not.

⛪ Basilica

Often free. The security line is the ticket. Knees covered, shoulders covered — they will send you out. Death holding an hourglass is in here, not a brochure.

🖼️ Museums

A hallway that does not end, then the chapel. Book weeks ahead. Coopculture is for the Colosseum. Wrong window.

🎨 Sistine Chapel

The end of the museum. No photos. Silence they will shush. Neck up. You will not keep a frame. That is the point.

👗 Dress code

Not a suggestion. No shorts, no bare shoulders. A scarf in the bag is the whole plan. I watched people get turned around at the door.

🚶 The day

Morning museums, afternoon basilica — or the reverse if your ticket says so. This is not a week. Ottaviano, walk, done.

🎟️ Vatican Museums

Official site only. Weeks, not a mood. The chapel is inside this ticket. Last Sunday free is a crush, not a hack.

That site is Rome’s archaeological parks. It will not get you into the museums. If the URL is not museivaticani, keep walking.

🚫 Not on the square

Eat before you enter or after you leave. The colonnade is not a picnic. Security will agree.

🛏️ Stay in Rome

There is no useful “Vatican hotel.” You are sleeping in Rome. Prati / Ottaviano if you want the morning walk. Centro or Trastevere if you want the actual city at night.

📅 The day

One morning and an afternoon. Do not give this a week. Book the museums, cover your knees, walk from Ottaviano.

💶 Spend less

A room with a view of a wall that is technically the Vatican is still a Rome room with a markup.

🌸 April–June

15–25°C. Peak lines. Book the museums anyway. Wednesday is the audience — skip it unless that is the point.

☀️ July–August

28–35°C. Stone that radiates. Early slot. Water. The dress code does not care that you are melting.

🍂 September–November

12–22°C. Kinder light, still a queue. One of the two best windows.

❄️ December–March

6–14°C. Shorter lines if you avoid the holidays. A coat that covers the shoulders solves two problems.
