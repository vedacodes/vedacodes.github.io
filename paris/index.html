Not a fairytale.
A city that works on you.

Paris captured me in the small hours: croissant steam, a street musician, light on Haussmann stone. The monuments are loud. The city is quieter than the posters. Walking the Seine at dusk, getting lost in the Louvre, climbing to Sacré-Cœur — that’s what stayed, not the slogan.

Intel

The useful layer — tickets to book, a croissant that actually flakes, and how not to melt in August.

🗼 Eiffel Tower

Book the summit online. Sunset on the Trocadéro steps, then stay for the sparkle. The hourly light show is not a myth.

🎨 Louvre

Timed entry, weeks ahead in peak season. Start with what you actually want. The Mona Lisa is a crowd with a painting attached.

⛪ Notre-Dame

The cathedral is open again. Timed reservation, often free. Walk Île de la Cité. Sainte-Chapelle is next door if you still have eyes for glass.

🏛️ Arc de Triomphe

Climb for the Champs-Élysées from above. Use the underground passage. The roundabout is not a crosswalk.

🤍 Sacré-Cœur

Funicular if your legs have already filed a complaint. Place du Tertre early, or you’ll be in someone else’s portrait.

🌊 Seine

Walk it. A cruise is optional. Evening light does more work than a dinner boat.

🗼 Eiffel Tower

Timed tickets on the official site. Summit sells out. Stairs to the second floor if you like a queue with a view.

📅 When to book

2–3 months ahead for April–June and September. 4–6 weeks for winter. Fashion Week and Christmas: book the second you know you’re going.

📍 Where

Saint-Germain — Left Bank, walkable, you pay for it. Le Marais — evenings, food, actual streets. Montmartre — the hill, the view, the stairs. Near the tower — the postcard, and the prices that come with it.

💶 Spend less

Skip the hotels glued to the tower. A small hotel on a residential street. Sunday–Thursday is kinder. Petit déjeuner included is not a small line item.

🌸 April–June

15–22°C, gardens, café weather. Peak prices. Still the easiest Paris to love.

☀️ July–August

20–26°C. Long days, Paris Plages, and a city half on vacation. Crowded. Go if you must; hide at noon.

🍂 September–November

12–20°C, gold trees, fewer people after the rentrée. One of the two best windows.

❄️ December–March

3–8°C, short days, Christmas lights, café interiors. Pack a coat. Cozy, if you like gray.

🚶 Walk

Central Paris is a pedestrian argument you will lose, happily. Cobbles. Shoes, water, done.

Free

🚇 Metro

It covers the city. Roughly 5:30am–1:15am (later on weekends). Ticket t+ or tap a Navigo Easy / contactless card. Machines in every station, tabacs, or the Île-de-France Mobilités app.

🚌 Bus

Same ticket as the metro. Above ground, so you actually see the city. Night buses after the metro sleeps. Route 69 is a sightseeing accident.
